# Results

Produced by `bash scripts/run_experiments.sh` (stage 1, CPU) on 2026-09-06, Windows 11,
Python 3.12, seed 7. Every table below is copied from a file in [`experiments/`](experiments/)
and every file is regenerated by the script; the whole CPU stage takes about 70 s. The
real-model stage (stage 2, `--model hf`, one RTX 4070) ran on 2026-09-07; its results are in
section 8 and the sections before it say where the scripted numbers stop and the real ones
begin.

| | |
|---|---|
| Corpus | `advicedoc corpus generate --n-per-type 60 --n-soa 120 --seed 7`: 660 real PDFs (120 Statements of Advice + 60 of each of 9 other types), gold JSON per document, a platform holdings snapshot per SoA, `manifest.jsonl`; 3.9 s to generate, byte-identical on re-run |
| Classifier split | stratified 75/25 by type, seed 0: 495 train / 165 test (30 SoA, 15 of each other type) |
| Models run here | TF-IDF + logistic regression (sigmoid and isotonic calibration), the rules extractor, the **scripted** LLM backend (answers derived from the gold with a known corruption rate), the review router on those features, the reconciliation simulator |
| Models run in stage 2 (section 8) | Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct through the in-process HF backend on one RTX 4070 |

Two kinds of number appear below and they must not be confused: **measurements** of
deterministic components against ground truth (classifier, rules extractor, reconciliation),
and **mechanism checks** where the scripted model's error rate is chosen by us, so the number
says that the evaluator, validators, retries, re-asks and router behave as designed — not how
good any model is.

## 1. Corpus

[`corpus_generate.json`](experiments/corpus_generate.json). Ten document types, three layout
styles (Helvetica / Times / Courier; `12 March 2026` / `12/03/2026` / `2026-03-12`;
`$1,250.00` / `AUD 1250` / `1,250 dollars`; numbered or plain headings; tables or prose),
two page arrangements per type, and distractors (an SoA that mentions a Fee Disclosure
Statement, a letter that quotes a product, a risk section that describes a second profile "by
comparison", a product "considered but not recommended"). SoAs run 4–8 pages. The rendered
PDFs are byte-identical across runs (`rl_config.invariant`), so gold *and* PDFs are
reproducible. The holdings simulator planted 57 discrepancies in 43 of the 120 SoAs
(16 `not_implemented`, 13 `amount_mismatch`, 19 `unexpected_product`, 9 `fee_mismatch`) and
left 77 clean; every implemented balance carries unclamped market noise (sd 2.5 %).

## 2. Document classifier

[`classifier_report.md`](experiments/classifier_report.md) (sigmoid) and
[`classifier_report_isotonic.md`](experiments/classifier_report_isotonic.md); the pipeline is
PDF → `pdfplumber` → first two pages → word 1–2-gram + char 3–5-gram TF-IDF + a 36-value dense
block → logistic regression → `CalibratedClassifierCV` (3 folds).

|---|---:|---:|
| Accuracy [95 % bootstrap CI] | 1.000 [1.000, 1.000] | 1.000 [1.000, 1.000] |
| Macro-F1 [95 % CI] | 1.000 [1.000, 1.000] | 1.000 [1.000, 1.000] |
| ECE (10 bins) | 0.064 | 0.001 |
| Mean confidence in the [0.9, 1.0] bin (accuracy 1.000) | 0.938 (n = 160) | 1.000 (n = 164) |
| Abstain rate at threshold 0.90 | 3.0 % | 0.6 % |
| Latency per document | 1.0 ms | 0.9 ms |

Every class has precision = recall = 1.000 (support 15, SoA 30). Two things are worth more than
the 1.000:

- **Calibration is a real difference between two models with identical accuracy.** Platt
  scaling on perfectly separable folds is under-confident (ECE 0.064, confidence 0.94 for an
  accuracy of 1.0); isotonic calibration is essentially exact. The abstain threshold has to be
  set on the *calibrated* scale — with the sigmoid model a threshold of 0.95 abstains on every
  document, with the isotonic model on 0.6 %.
- **OCR-like noise** (seeded character confusions and dropped spaces on the extracted text):

| Character noise rate | Accuracy (sigmoid) | Macro-F1 (sigmoid) | ECE | Abstain rate | Accuracy (isotonic) | ECE (isotonic) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.05 | 0.982 [0.958, 1.000] | 0.982 [0.954, 1.000] | 0.081 | 3.6 % | 0.994 [0.982, 1.000] | 0.019 |
| 0.10 | 0.958 [0.921, 0.988] | 0.959 [0.923, 0.984] | 0.127 | 10.3 % | 0.964 [0.933, 0.988] | 0.037 |

**Zero-shot LLM baseline (mechanism check).** The scripted backend answers with the gold type
except with probability 0.2, when it returns a wrong type, prose, or truncated JSON:
accuracy 0.818 [0.758, 0.873], 4 unparseable answers recorded as *missing*, 64 JSON repairs,
0 / 30 discordant pairs against the ML model, exact McNemar p < 0.001. The number is the
corruption rate we chose; what it verifies is that the comparison path (repair, missing-as-
missing, McNemar on the same documents) works. The real zero-shot accuracy of Qwen3-4B and
Qwen2.5-1.5B against the same 165 documents is in section 8 (both 1.000; the ML model wins on
cost and calibration, not accuracy).

**Metadata rules** (client names, adviser, document date from labelled first-page fields,
letter conventions and the first date): 1.000 on each field for all ten types on the 165
test documents (table at the end of the classifier report).

What 1.000 does and does not say: the corpus is templated and every type states what it is on
page 1, so the classifier separates the ten types the way a production classifier separates
clean platform documents; the noise rows are the informative ones, and scanned client-supplied
documents will be harder than anything here.

## 3. Rules extractor on the 120 gold SoAs

[`extraction_rules.md`](experiments/extraction_rules.md). Text comes from the rendered PDFs
through `pdfplumber` (words clustered into lines, `extract_tables`); the extractor detects
sections by heading (exact, then fuzzy for short lines), parses the recommendation /
replacement / fee tables with a prose fallback, and canonicalises product names against the
product master with `rapidfuzz`.

| Input | Doc-level accuracy [95 % CI] | Mean field accuracy | Recommendations F1 | Replacements F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| clean PDF text | **1.000 [1.000, 1.000]** | 1.000 [1.000, 1.000] | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| + 2 % character noise | 0.158 [0.100, 0.225] | 0.814 [0.787, 0.838] | 0.840 [0.795, 0.881] | 0.714 [0.634, 0.793] |
| + 5 % character noise | 0.008 [0.000, 0.025] | 0.603 [0.569, 0.634] | 0.641 [0.588, 0.695] | 0.397 [0.314, 0.478] |
| + 10 % character noise | 0.000 [0.000, 0.000] | 0.365 [0.342, 0.389] | 0.360 [0.301, 0.420] | 0.214 [0.150, 0.285] |

Per field, the fragile ones under 5 % noise are the tables (recommendations 0.225,
replacements 0.342, platform admin fee 0.358) and the money fields (initial fee 0.533,
ongoing fee 0.517); the robust ones are enumerations and labelled short fields (risk profile
0.875, authority signed 0.875, ongoing fee percent 0.808). Sections are still found under
noise thanks to the fuzzy heading match (fees missing in 0 / 1 / 2 documents at 2 / 5 / 10 %,
recommendations in 0 / 0 / 1), so the losses are inside the sections, not in chunking. The
validators see part of the damage — `missing_field` 46 / 122 / 244 and `fee_arithmetic`
12 / 19 / 6 violations at the three noise rates — but not all of it, which is the point of
section 5.

Reading: on clean text a careful rules extractor is exact on this corpus and costs 1 ms per
document; it degrades fast under OCR-like noise. That is the baseline an LLM strategy has to
beat *on noisy or unfamiliar layouts*, and it is why `docs/experiments` keeps the noisy rows.

## 4. LLM extraction with the scripted backend — mechanism checks

[`extraction_fake_c00.md`](experiments/extraction_fake_c00.md),
[`extraction_fake_c01.md`](experiments/extraction_fake_c01.md),
[`extraction_fake_c03.md`](experiments/extraction_fake_c03.md). The scripted model reads the
document id and section name from the prompt and answers from the gold; with probability
`corruption` per section it returns a wrong amount, a dropped item, an invalid enum, an unknown
product, a shifted date, an empty reason or truncated JSON, and 20 % of its good answers come
fenced in ```` ```json ````. Seven prompts per SoA (header, scope, risk, recommendations,
replacement, fees, authority); one parse retry per section; `llm_validated` re-asks a section
at most once when a hard validation fails.

Paired on the same 120 documents (`llm_validated` − `llm`, document-level correctness):
+0.092 [+0.050, +0.150], 11 / 0 discordant pairs, McNemar p = 0.001 at corruption 0.1;
+0.150 [+0.091, +0.217], 18 / 0, p < 0.001 at 0.3. `rules` beats both at every corruption
level — that is the corruption we injected, not a statement about a model.

What the checks establish:

- **Exactness at zero corruption**: 801 calls, 154 repairs (the 19 % fenced answers), 0 parse
  failures, every field recovered — the prompts, JSON repair, pydantic validation and merge are
  lossless.
- **Nothing is silently accepted**: truncated JSON becomes a parse failure, a bounded retry
  (one per section: 19 / 56 retries for 20 / 61 failures), and if that fails a *missing*
  section with a recorded reason. The 25 "missing authority" and 14 "missing replacement"
  sections at every level are documents that genuinely have no such section (gold `None` /
  empty), reported rather than defaulted.
- **Validators catch what they can check and re-asks fix most of it**: at corruption 0.3 the
  hard violations on the plain `llm` run are `fee_arithmetic` 16, `fee_basis_inconsistent` 12,
  `missing_field` 31, `date_implausible` 5, `product_unknown` 3,
  `amount_exceeds_available_funds` 3; `llm_validated` re-asked 58 sections and cleared 44
  (the scripted model corrects itself with probability 0.7 on a re-ask, so 44 / 58 = 76 % is the
  expected rate). Per field the validators repair `fees.ongoing_fee_basis` 0.942 → 1.000,
  `fees.ongoing_fee_percent` 0.958 → 1.000, `advice_date` 0.858 → 0.975, `licensee`
  0.900 → 0.983 — and leave `risk_profile` (0.867), `scope` (0.750) and
  `authority_to_proceed_signed` (0.850) exactly where they were, because a *wrong but valid*
  enum passes every deterministic check. That residual is what the router in section 5 is for.
- Latency and "tokens per document" (≈ 1,300 words of prompt + answer) are the scripted
  model's and only show the accounting works. Real latency, token counts, repair and re-ask
  rates and the rules-vs-llm-vs-llm_validated paired comparison for Qwen3-4B and
  Qwen2.5-1.5B are in section 8.

## 5. Review router — risk-coverage (mechanism check)

[`router_report.md`](experiments/router_report.md) and
[`router_report_noise005.md`](experiments/router_report_noise005.md). One record per critical
field (risk profile, each recommendation, the two advice fees, each replacement) of the
`llm_validated` extraction at corruption 0.3, labelled correct/incorrect against the gold;
features: agreement with the rules extractor, validator flags, fuzzy product score, JSON
repairs and parse failures, section-found flag, length and magnitude z-scores, the model's
self-reported confidence. Logistic regression → isotonic calibration; all probabilities below
are out-of-fold (`GroupKFold` by document, 5 folds); a document is routed to review when the
minimum P(correct) over its fields is below τ.

| | Clean text | 5 % character noise |
|---|---:|---:|
| Fields / documents | 969 / 120 | 972 / 120 |
| Base field error / base document error | 4.2 % / 31.7 % | 4.4 % / 30.8 % |
| ECE of P(field correct) | 0.000 | 0.015 |
| Area under the risk-coverage curve [95 % CI] (lower is better) | 0.058 [0.033, 0.095] | 0.118 [0.058, 0.204] |
| τ for ≤ 1 % residual document error | 0.984 | 1.000 |
| Review rate at that τ [95 % CI] | **31.7 % [24.2, 40.0]** | 99.2 % [97.5, 100] |
| Residual error at that τ | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| Naive: review everything | 100 % review, 0 residual | same |
| Naive: review nothing | 0 % review, 31.7 % residual | 0 %, 30.8 % |
| Naive: review iff any hard validator violation | 9.2 % review, **30.3 % residual** | 15.8 %, 30.7 % |

- **Clean text is an oracle case.** The rules extractor is exact here, so "agrees with rules"
  (coefficient +2.30, the next largest +0.26) identifies every wrong field; the router reviews
  exactly the 31.7 % of documents that contain an error and nothing else, ECE 0.000. It proves
  the selective-prediction machinery (calibration, min-over-fields, curve, τ search, naive
  baselines, CIs) end to end; it does not measure a hard problem.
- **Noisy text is the informative case.** With the rules extractor at 60 % field accuracy the
  agreement feature is no longer an oracle (coefficients: agree_rules +1.87, amount_log +1.70,
  self_conf +1.19 — the scripted confidence is deliberately only weakly informative,
  validator_warning +0.64), the reliability table spreads over every bin, and the curve is a
  real trade-off: reviewing 25 % of documents leaves 14.4 % residual error, 50 % leaves 3.3 %,
  70 % leaves 2.8 %, and the 1 % target is only reached by reviewing 99.2 %. Against the naive
  "review if a validator fails" policy (15.8 % review, 30.7 % residual) the router is far
  better at every review rate above ~15 %, and it still cannot reach 1 % because the corruption
  types that pass every validator (wrong-but-valid enums, dropped list items) leave no feature
  trace in this set-up.
- The router on *real* `llm_validated` extractions (Qwen3-4B, then the 1.5B baseline) — τ for
  1 % residual and the review rate it implies, the curve, ECE, coefficients — is in section 8.
  That is the number a product owner would act on (27.5 % review for 0 % residual with the
  4 B model); the two above are the machinery.

## 6. Advice-vs-implementation reconciliation

[`reconcile_report.md`](experiments/reconcile_report.md): each of the 120 gold SoAs against its
simulated platform snapshot 90 days later; a per-client markdown example is in
[`reconcile_example_soa_0007.md`](experiments/reconcile_example_soa_0007.md).

| Amount tolerance 5 % | Planted | Detected | Detection rate [95 % CI] | Documents with a false alarm | False-alarm rate per document [95 % CI] |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| not_implemented | 16 | 16 | 1.000 [1.000, 1.000] | 0 | 0.000 |
| amount_mismatch | 13 | 13 | 1.000 [1.000, 1.000] | 16 | 0.133 [0.075, 0.192] |
| unexpected_product | 19 | 19 | 1.000 [1.000, 1.000] | 0 | 0.000 |
| fee_mismatch | 9 | 9 | 1.000 [1.000, 1.000] | 0 | 0.000 |

Overall detection 57 / 57 = 1.000 [1.000, 1.000]; 13.0 % [6.5, 20.8] of the 77 clean documents
raise a discrepancy — all of them `amount_mismatch` from market noise beyond 5 % (sd 2.5 %
unclamped, so 4.6 % of faithfully implemented balances land outside ±5 %). At a 10 % tolerance
the false alarms vanish (0 clean documents flagged) and detection of `amount_mismatch` drops to
11 / 13 = 0.846 [0.625, 1.000] because the two planted ±8 % mismatches are inside the band;
overall 0.965 [0.905, 1.000]. The trade-off is the tolerance parameter, and the simulator is
how it is chosen rather than guessed. Feeding the reconciliation from the rules *extraction*
instead of the gold gives identical numbers on clean text (the extraction is exact).

Two simulator bugs were found by this evaluation and fixed before these numbers were written:
an "unexpected" product could be one the advice expected (it then read as an amount mismatch),
and a `not_implemented` victim could be the unexpected product planted a moment earlier
(contradictory ground truth). Both are the kind of thing a known-truth simulator exists to
catch.

## 8. Real models — Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct

`SKIP_CPU=1 GPU_LIMIT=40 bash scripts/run_experiments.sh` on one RTX 4070 (12 GB), bf16, greedy
decoding through the in-process `transformers` backend (≈ 14 tok/s for the 4 B model). The
extraction runs use the first 40 of the 120 gold SoAs; zero-shot classification uses the full

evaluation re-uses both. Wall clock: 4 B 19 min (classification 2.5 min, extraction 16 min,
router 2 s), 1.5 B 15 min.

### What stage 2 changed in the picture

- The templated corpus is easy enough that both models classify it perfectly; the ML
  classifier wins on cost and calibration, not accuracy. Scanned or unfamiliar layouts are
  where the two would separate, and that is stated as a limitation, not measured here.
- A 4 B model reads structured advice documents well — every scalar field exact on 40
  documents — and fails on a boolean and a list, i.e. on *interpretation*, not on reading.
  The validators are blind to both; the calibrated router is not.
- Model size matters more than the prompt: the 1.5 B model could not produce one fully
  correct document, and its re-asks fixed 10 of 46 sections against 6 of 7 for the 4 B.
- Latency: 23 s per SoA on a consumer GPU (7 section prompts, no batching), cached re-use for
  the validated pass and the router, so a re-run of the whole stage is free.

## 9. Limitations of these numbers

- Synthetic corpus with a known truth is what makes CIs, calibration tables and detection /
  false-alarm rates possible; it also makes the clean-text classifier and rules numbers
  optimistic. Layout variety is three styles and two arrangements per type, not the long tail
  of real licensee templates.
- The scripted LLM produces the corruption *distribution we designed* (uniform over seven modes
  per section); a real model's errors are correlated with the text and with each other. That is
  exactly why the mechanism-check sections say "mechanism check" and why stage 2 exists.
- OCR noise is character-level and independent; real OCR errors cluster by font, table rule and
  scan quality. The pipeline is OCR-agnostic by construction (any OCR that returns lines and
  tables plugs into `ingest.Document`), but nothing here has seen a real scan.
- Stage 2 uses 40 of the 120 SoAs for extraction (a GPU budget, not a sampling design) and
  one consumer GPU without batching; the latencies are honest for that setup and pessimistic
  for a served model.
