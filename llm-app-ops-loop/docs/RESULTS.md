# Results

Produced by `bash scripts/run_experiments.sh` (CPU stage) on 2026-09-06, Windows 11, Python
3.12, no GPU and no model download: the demo application, the judge and the regression-gate
model are the scripted backends. Every artefact is in [`experiments/`](experiments/) and is
regenerated by the script; traffic is byte-identical for a seed. The real-model stage
(`STAGE=gpu`, Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 on one RTX 4070) ran on 2026-09-07; section 8 has its
results and what they changed.

| Baseline behaviour (steady, v1) | error rate ≈ 0.3 %, refusal rate ≈ 3 % (out-of-scope questions), negative feedback ≈ 4 %, 1–1.5 % guardrail blocks (a TFN in the question), heuristic quality mean ≈ 0.98, cost ≈ $0.00029 per request, latency p95 ≈ 1.6 s |

## 1. Monitor self-evaluation — the headline

`opsloop monitor evaluate --scenarios … --seeds 1 2 3 --minutes 240 --gate`
([monitor_evaluation.md](experiments/monitor_evaluation.md), [.json](experiments/monitor_evaluation.json)).
Each run plants one incident; the monitor is evaluated every 5 simulated minutes. *Detected*
means an alert of the kind's expected family fired between the incident start and its end plus
the longest window (120 min); *time-to-detect* is the first such tick minus the start. A tick is
*quiet* when the shortest long window is full (≥ 10 min), no incident is active and none ended
within the last 120 minutes; any alert on a quiet tick is a false alarm. Intervals are seeded
bootstraps (1 000 resamples) over seeds and over quiet ticks.

| Incident kind | Magnitude | Expected alert(s) | Runs | Detected [95 % CI] | Time-to-detect, min: mean [95 % CI] (values) |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|
| error_burst | 25 % timeouts / 5xx + 5 % tool errors, 30 min | `error_rate` | 3 | 1.00 [1, 1] | 5.0 [5, 5] (5, 5, 5) |
| pii_leak | tool exposes the TFN, model echoes it, 40 min | `pii_leak_rate` | 3 | 1.00 [1, 1] | 5.0 [5, 5] (5, 5, 5) |
| regressed_prompt | v2-regressed active, 60 min | `quality_mean`, `grounding_rate`, `refusal_rate`, `json_validity` | 3 | 1.00 [1, 1] | 6.7 [5, 10] (5, 10, 5) |
| cost_creep | completion tokens × 4–5, 90 min | `cost_per_request` | 3 | 1.00 [1, 1] | 8.3 [5, 10] (10, 10, 5) |
| latency_spike | LLM latency × 2, 40 min | `latency_p95` | 3 | 1.00 [1, 1] | 13.3 [10, 20] (10, 10, 20) |
| topic_shift | 35 % out-of-scope questions, 120 min | `topic_drift` | 3 | 1.00 [1, 1] | 13.3 [5, 20] (15, 20, 5) |

**False alarms: 1 of 459 quiet ticks = 0.22 % [0.00 %, 0.66 %]** (one `topic_drift` on the
steady run, seed 1). The gate (`detection ≥ 0.9 for every kind and false alarms ≤ 5 %`) passes;
it is the same command CI runs.

Reading the table:

* With three seeds the detection intervals are degenerate (every run detected). The harness
  takes `--seeds 1 2 3 4 5 …`; on four seeds during development every kind was still 4/4 with
  1/612 false alarms.
* Time-to-detect is quantised to the 5-minute tick. Error bursts, PII leaks and the regressed
  prompt are caught on the first tick because the page window (10 min) fills with bad events
  immediately (25 % errors ≈ 12 in ten minutes). Latency and cost creep need the long window's
  bad fraction to reach 3× budget, hence 10 minutes; the one 20-minute latency detection is a
  seed whose first ten minutes of spike drew fewer slow requests.
* Topic shift is detected by the novel-share test (baseline novel share 6–9 % → 20–25 %
  during the shift); the omnibus chi-square/JS test alone detected 2 of 3 seeds and 35–60
  minutes late during development, which is why the targeted test exists.
* Other alerts also fire during incidents (listed per run in the report): a regressed prompt
  raises `negative_feedback_rate` later, a topic shift raises `refusal_rate` and
  `quality_mean` (out-of-scope questions are refused by design). They are not false alarms and
  they are useful — the runbook says which one explains the others.

What was fixed because the harness measured it (each a false-alarm source in an earlier run):
windows evaluated before they were full (5-minute-old 2-hour windows), a 1 % error budget on a
10-minute window paging on two Poisson errors (now ≥ 3 bad events), the refusal-rate floor
evaluated on a short window, guardrail-blocked JSON requests counted as invalid JSON, and
30-second timeouts counted as slow requests (latency now counts successful requests only).

Per-run alert tables from `opsloop monitor run`: [alerts_error_burst.md](experiments/alerts_error_burst.md)
(the `page` window fires at minute 105 for an incident starting at 100, escalates to `fast`
then `slow` as the longer windows fill, and clears 10 minutes after the burst ends on the page
window while the slow window still reports it) and
[alerts_regressed_prompt.md](experiments/alerts_regressed_prompt.md).

## 2. Quality loop on production traces

**Judge** (`opsloop judge`, fake judge, [judge_uniform.json](experiments/judge_uniform.json),
[judge_stratified.json](experiments/judge_stratified.json)) on the steady run, seed 1:

| Sample | n | Missing | Helpfulness | Grounding | Tone | Overall (1–5) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| uniform, budget 140 | 140 | 0 | 4.77 | 4.66 | 4.89 | 4.77 |
| stratified (errors, negative feedback, low score, then random), budget 40 | 35 | 0 | 3.34 | 4.20 | 4.31 | 3.95 |

The stratified sample scores 0.8 points lower on the same population — the selection bias
that made the first smoke run raise a `judge_score` alert. Only uniform-sample scores feed the
judged-quality SLO; both are stored with the judge model (`northshore-judge-8b`) and judge
prompt version (`judge-v1`). Every fake-judge reply is fenced JSON, so `repairs` equals the
number of calls; with `--judge-invalid-rate 0.05` on the stratified run the truncated replies
were repaired or recorded as missing (0 missing here because the fake judge's failure draw is
seeded per answer and both attempts agree).

## 3. Prompt regression gate (fake model, fake judge, dataset v2 = 205 cases)

`opsloop regress --dataset adviser_assistant@v2 --candidate @v2 --baseline @v1 --model fake --use-judge --gate`
([regression_gate_v2_good.md](experiments/regression_gate_v2_good.md),
[regression_gate_v2_regressed_bad.md](experiments/regression_gate_v2_regressed_bad.md)).
Per-case score = ½ heuristic quality + ½ judge (1–5 rescaled to 0–1); margin 0.05, slice
margin 0.15, JSON floor 0.95, cost budget 1.5× baseline.

| Candidate vs v1 | Mean cand / base | Δ [95 % CI] | P(Δ > 0) | wins / losses / ties | Pass–fail: cand-only / base-only (McNemar p) | JSON validity | Cost / case | Judge cand / base | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v2 | 0.960 / 0.938 | +0.022 [+0.007, +0.038] | 1.00 | 18 / 21 / 166 | 18 / 0 (p ≈ 0) | 8/8 vs 8/8 | $0.000324 vs $0.000299 (×1.09) | 4.72 / 4.61 | **PASS** |
| v2-regressed | 0.634 / 0.938 | −0.304 [−0.331, −0.277] | 0.00 | 0 / 186 / 19 | 0 / 123 (p < 10⁻⁴) | 0/8 vs 8/8 | $0.000282 vs $0.000299 (×0.94) | 2.96 / 4.61 | **FAIL** |

Slices worth reading in the good report: `fees` +0.199 (v2 quotes the fee percentage exactly
where v1 wrote "about 1.5 %"), `fee_pct` and `fds_summary` +0.375, `reviews` **−0.104**
(v2's date-only review answers drop the courtesy check — inside the 0.15 slice margin,
reported, not failing). The 21 v2 "losses" on the paired score are those review cases; the 18
wins are the fee cases that now pass grounding. The bad report fails on non-inferiority,
McNemar, 25 of 34 slices and the JSON floor (every JSON answer truncated by the 40-word cap);
its cost is *lower* — a cheaper prompt is not a better one.

## 4. Canary: advance vs automatic rollback

`opsloop release start --version … --stage 10`, then rounds of 8 simulated hours of
`demo traffic --use-release` (200 sessions) followed by `release auto --window 8h --apply`
([canary_canary_good_round*.json](experiments/), [canary_canary_bad_round0.json](experiments/canary_canary_bad_round0.json),
[releases_after_*.yaml](experiments/)). Policy: ≥ 100 requests per cohort; error and refusal
rates by one-sided two-proportion test (Δ > 0.02 / 0.05 at α = 0.05); quality mean by bootstrap
(upper bound of Δ < −0.05 rolls back).

| Canary | Stage | Canary n / control n | Error rate Δ (p) | Refusal rate Δ (p) | Quality Δ [95 % CI] | Decision |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| v2 | 10 % | 174 / 2 265 | +0.001 (0.44) | −0.002 (0.56) | +0.012 [+0.000, +0.020] | advance → 50 % |
| v2 | 50 % | 1 027 / 1 402 | −0.001 (0.67) | −0.006 (0.80) | +0.014 [+0.008, +0.020] | advance → 100 % |
| v2 | 100 % | 2 486 / 3 663 (historical) | −0.001 (0.71) | −0.004 (0.83) | +0.011 [+0.008, +0.015] | **promote** (v2 becomes active) |
| v2-regressed | 10 % | 188 / 2 161 | −0.001 (0.66) | **+0.122 (< 10⁻⁴)** | **−0.217 [−0.236, −0.197]** | **rollback** |

At 100 % there is no concurrent control cohort any more, so the last stage compares the canary
with the historical control — every earlier request served by the active version (3 663 here).
The history of each decision is appended to `releases.yaml`
([releases_after_canary_good.yaml](experiments/releases_after_canary_good.yaml): start → advance
→ advance → promote; [releases_after_canary_bad.yaml](experiments/releases_after_canary_bad.yaml):
start → rollback with the two reasons).

## 5. Replay and incident report

[replay_same_prompt.md](experiments/replay_same_prompt.md): a `fee_pct` trace from the
error-burst run replayed with its recorded prompt (v1, hash `238432dcc901`) and the fake model
reproduces the recorded answer exactly (similarity 1.000, quality 0.7 → 0.7, grounded
false → false: v1's "about 1.5 %"). [replay_with_v2.md](experiments/replay_with_v2.md): the
same trace with `--prompt-version v2` answers "1.49 %", grounded true, quality 1.0, with the
unified diff and the note that the prompt hash changed since recording.

[incident_report_error_burst.md](experiments/incident_report_error_burst.md) over 01:00–03:00
of the error-burst run (621 requests, 110 with at least one failure): the timeline shows the
failure rate jumping from 9–18 % to 37–40 % at 01:40 and back to 12 % at 02:10; the elevated
period is located as 01:30–02:10 (failure rate 33.9 % inside vs 10.5 % outside, z = 7.0);
availability failures rose from 0.2 % to 20.3 % of requests, so the suspected cause is
provider availability; tool span errors (`get_holdings` ×3) and five example trace ids are
listed; the follow-ups are the runbook's. The 10 % background failure rate is v1's known
behaviour (rounded fee percentages, out-of-scope refusals, negative feedback), which is why the
cause is decided on the *increase* during the elevated period rather than on window totals.

## 8. Real-model stage — Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 answering *and* judging

`MODEL=D:/models/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 STAGE=gpu GPU_LIMIT=80 bash scripts/run_experiments.sh`
on one RTX 4070 (12 GB), bf16, greedy, ≈ 14 tok/s. The first 80 cases of dataset v2 (a GPU
budget: 205 cases × 2 prompts × answer + judge is ≈ 3 h at this speed), one in-process model
shared by the answerer and the judge (two copies spill out of 12 GB and run three times
slower — measured, then fixed), every greedy answer cached per request so the v1 baseline
half of the second gate came from the first. Wall clock: v1 vs v2 19.4 min, v1 vs
v2-regressed 7.5 min (baseline cached), 60 judged traces 4.1 min, replay 15 s.

### Regression gate with a real model ([`regression_gate_hf_v2.md`](experiments/regression_gate_hf_v2.md), [`regression_gate_hf_v2_regressed.md`](experiments/regression_gate_hf_v2_regressed.md))

| | v1 → v2 | v1 → v2-regressed |
|---|---|---|
| **Decision** | **FAIL** — JSON-validity floor | **FAIL** — McNemar and JSON-validity floor |
| Paired quality Δ (candidate − baseline), 80 cases | +0.009 [−0.000, +0.020], 26 wins / 11 losses / 43 ties | −0.028 [−0.041, −0.015], 4 / 48 / 28 |
| Pass–fail (exact McNemar) | candidate-only 2, baseline-only 1, p = 1.0 | candidate-only 3, baseline-only 14, **p = 0.013** |
| Slices (9, margin 0.15) | all OK; contributions +0.032, next_review +0.030 | all inside the margin (allocation −0.066, fees −0.058) |
| JSON validity on the 2 cases that require JSON | **0 / 2 candidate, 0 / 2 baseline** | 0 / 2, 0 / 2 |
| Completion tokens (mean) | 81.7 vs 80.2 | **34.9** vs 80.2 |
| Latency p95 | 13.5 s vs 8.1 s | 2.8 s vs 8.1 s |
| Cost per case | *missing* (no price for a local model) | *missing* |

Two things the scripted run could not show:

* **The good prompt did not pass.** Quality is non-inferior and slightly better, every slice
  is fine, and the gate still says FAIL, because the two curated cases that require a JSON
  answer got prose from the 4 B model under *both* prompts. The floor is absolute (≥ 0.95 on
  the candidate), which is right for a gate — a prompt that cannot produce JSON where JSON
  is contractually expected must not ship — and the report names the reason so the fix is
  obvious (a format instruction the model actually follows, or a JSON-mode backend). A
  floor measured on two cases is under-powered and the report prints `n=2` next to it; the
  honest reading is "instruction-following defect in the prompt family", not "v2 is worse".
* **The regressed prompt regressed by a tenth of what the fake said.** −0.028 against
  −0.304: the scripted model applies the regression directive literally, the real model
  mostly answers well regardless and only gets terser (35 tokens against 80). The gate
  still catches it — the interval excludes zero, McNemar is significant, the JSON floor
  fails — so the machinery is doing its job on a subtle regression, which is the case that
  matters in production.

### Real judge on production traces ([`judge_hf_sample.json`](experiments/judge_hf_sample.json))

60 uniformly sampled `steady` traces: 60 scored, **0 missing, 0 JSON repairs**; helpfulness
4.73, grounding 4.73, tone 4.90, overall **4.79 / 5** — within 0.02 of the fake judge's
uniform-sample mean (4.77, section 2), with rationales that cite the tool output
("each holding's name, value and weight percentage are directly taken from the
`top_holdings` array"). The judge prompt survives contact with a real model; what it cannot
tell us yet is its agreement with human review, which the feedback endpoint exists to
collect.

### Replay ([`replay_hf.md`](experiments/replay_hf.md))

The `total_fees` trace replays with the recorded prompt version (hash `238432dcc901`,
identical to the recorded one) and the recorded tool outputs; the real model's answer differs
in wording from the scripted one (similarity 0.41) but carries the same fee total
($5,151.63), passes numeric grounding and scores 1.0 — which is exactly what replay is for:
same inputs, a different model, a diff and a score rather than an opinion.

### What stage 2 changed in the picture

- The monitor self-evaluation (section 1) is unchanged by design: it measures the alerting
  policy on simulated traffic and a real model does not enter it.
- The regression gate's *floors* proved more decisive than its *statistics* with a real
  model: the paired test said "fine", the JSON floor said "no", and the floor was right.
- Scripted regressions are upper bounds. A real prompt regression is small and quiet — the
  reason the gate combines a paired interval, McNemar on pass–fail and per-slice checks
  rather than trusting one number.
- The GPU budget shaped the run: 80 cases, a response cache, one shared model instance. The
  cost column is *missing* rather than zero for a local model, on purpose.
